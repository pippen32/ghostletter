SUBJECT: The price you are afraid to say out loud

The price you are afraid to say out loud

Last spring, a client sat in my office, looked at her own proposal, and whispered the number — as if the price itself might be offended. She had ten years of results behind her. She still priced like she was asking for a favour.

Here is what I told her, and what I now tell every expert I work with: your price is not a request. It is information. It tells a buyer what kind of problem you solve, how seriously to take the work, and whether you believe your own track record.

When you discount before anyone asks, you are not being kind. You are training good clients to wait, and inviting bargain hunters who will drain the very hours you were trying to sell. The clients you actually want are rarely shopping for the cheapest option. They are shopping for certainty.

So we did one uncomfortable thing. We raised her fee by forty percent, removed two low-priced offers, and rewrote her proposal to lead with outcomes instead of hours. She lost two prospects that month. She signed three better ones the next — at the new price, without a negotiation call.

Try this before your next proposal: say the number once, plainly, then stop talking. Silence after a price is not awkward. It is professional.

If pricing conversations still make your stomach drop, reply to this email. I open two consulting spots each quarter, and we start exactly there — with the number you have been afraid to say out loud.

— Daniel Mercer, Mercer Advisory (fictional consultant, sample only)
